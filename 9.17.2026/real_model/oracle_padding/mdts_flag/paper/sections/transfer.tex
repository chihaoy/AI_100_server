\section{What Transfers}
\label{sec:transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:transfer} separates, for each part of \sys, the reasoning we expect to hold on other statically partitioned
targets from the conditions under which it pays and the compiler behavior that shaped it. We measured one model under one
compiler, so this section states expectations, not results.

\textbf{The method.} Capacities fixed before routing is known should follow per-device load ranks, and the aggregation
must be designed with them: its cost must not grow with the shapes the capacities create, and the graph must state device
ownership and token-local reduction in a form the compiler preserves. The order-statistics bound holds for any routing;
its stability across workloads is a property of the model.

\textbf{When it pays.} Padded rows must cost time: a 16-lane stage of ours streams its MXFP6 weights in about 0.6\,ms, stays
at that floor with 92 rows per lane and is bound by its tensor-unit time with 296, and the compiler skips padded rows only
in whole tiles. Kernels that size expert work at run time have no padding to remove \citep{gale2023megablocks}, and blocks
whose weight loads are shared across an expert's blocks \citep{awsneuronmoe} may close the gap we measured. Ranking needs
many experts per device relative to $k$: 32 per SoC against $k=8$ here, while 8 experts with $k=2$ on four devices would
leave little to rank.

\textbf{What the compiler shaped.} Which graph forms keep ownership is a property of this partitioner; another compiler may
keep index values or slices local, or need another form. We expect the requirement to transfer, that the graph let the
compiler see which device owns which rows, not the reshape that met it here. The attention rewrite and the dense-combine
repairs fix this export and compiler, and the production baseline reflects its default partitioning, which takes about as
long for 128 tokens as for 256: our speedups over production do not transfer, while the reasoning does. A scheduler that
overlaps independent stages would favor more tiers.
